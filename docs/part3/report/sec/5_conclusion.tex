\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked how far a frozen encoder carries few-shot industrial anomaly detection. A
verified AnomalyDINO reproduction anchors the visual route at 94.61 image AUROC on VisA's
six object-centric categories at 1-shot; a controlled encoder swap shows CLIP's
language-aligned vision tower gives up 5.6 points in dense patch matching -- a property of
the features, not of sample size; the arms disagree on 36.8\% of images, leaving room for
the pre-registered fusion to gain $+0.68$ points across nine (shot, seed) cells, at
calibration gaps reported as achieved operating points, not guarantees.

In practical terms, the trade-off is explicit. When latency or memory dominates, DINOv2
alone is the operating point (148.5\,ms, 120\,MiB per image), and the confirmed
DINOv2--CLIP fusion buys $+0.68$ points and a third lower cross-cell variance (both arms
run). When accuracy dominates and ${\sim}2.6$\,s and 24\,GB of peak memory per image are
acceptable, frozen forced-choice Qwen2-VL is the strongest single route on these
categories; its fusion with the visual arms and its calibration remain open. Neither
comparison extends beyond these six categories.

The wider lesson: with no training signal, every claim reduces to the representation or
the decision rule; both must be measured under a protocol frozen before scoring, with
per-image evidence for every aggregate and reliability next to accuracy. Benchmarks are no
longer the bottleneck~\cite{hecklerkram2026mvtec2}; representations and decisions are.
